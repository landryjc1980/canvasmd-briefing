# Readout Next: ideal-edition decisions

This is the product target derived from the live 2026-08-19 editions. It comes
before the shared Story contract. The goal is not a new taxonomy. The goal is a
brief that lets an oncologist see what happened, inspect the original evidence,
and understand the physician conversation without reading the same development
twice.

## Rules visible in every edition

1. One development occupies one top-story position. Coverage, physician posts,
   publisher posts, papers, and podcast moments about that development live under
   the same story as evidence.
2. The anchor says only what its authoritative source establishes. A paper is a
   paper; an FDA action is an FDA action; an episode is an episode.
3. `Primary endpoint met`, `primary endpoint not met`, and equivalent elevated
   labels require an explicit statement in the authoritative source. Hazard ratios,
   p-values, publication types, registry dates, and bibliography relationships do
   not mint those labels.
4. An uncertain trial relationship is omitted. The source remains visible without
   the inferred trial wrapper.
5. Physician conversation is a first-class layer, not a popularity footnote. The
   collapsed card shows who is discussing the development; expansion shows their
   actual words, threads, and amplification receipts.
6. Thin editions stay thin. No filler is added to reach five cards.

## GU

- Lead with the PSMAddition Lancet paper as a paper. It may carry an explicit
  primary-result statement only when quoted from the Lancet source.
- Keep the JAMA surveillance paper, LITESPARK-011 Lancet paper, APMS response paper,
  and ARPI-toxicity episode as distinct developments.
- Refuse to infer a trial relationship for the APMS response paper from nearby
  mentions or a registry acronym.

## GI

- Lead with the Nature Medicine daraxonrasib resistance paper as a paper.
- Do not call it a RASolute 302 readout; the confirmatory trial is future context,
  not the cohort reported by this paper.
- Keep histotripsy, NETTER-2 analysis, the ASCO guideline, and the pancreatic-cancer
  podcast as separate developments.

## Lung

- Keep the nivolumab biomarker paper, SKYSCRAPER-03 paper, LIBRETTO-432 paper,
  transformed-SCLC paper, and SCLC episode.
- The SCLC episode may contain only SCLC receipts and moments. NSCLC material is a
  different subject even when the same show or clinician is involved.
- Any endpoint label must be a source quotation, never a significance inference.

## Breast

- Keep the four materially different papers and the TNBC episode.
- Conversation belongs beneath each paper; clinician count is secondary to their
  attributable commentary.

## Heme

- Lead with the FDA iberdomide approval.
- Attach approval-specific physician posts, publisher coverage, and related
  interviews to that one event.
- Keep the CLL management paper, CRISPR/myeloid-cell paper, marrow-failure paper,
  and myeloma episode as separate developments.

## Skin

- Lead with the FDA RP1/vusolimogene plus nivolumab approval.
- Attach Toni Choueiri's approval post and all other approval-specific receipts to
  that event. The same post must not also occupy a standalone hero position.
- Keep the NRAS-mutant melanoma paper, skin-cancer podcast, and Merck/Moderna
  vaccine development as distinct stories.

## Gyn

- Keep the ovarian-aging paper and CHK-1 clinician thread when those are the only
  material developments.
- Do not pad the edition or promote weakly related material from another disease.

## What readers should understand without opening a drawer

- The literal event, paper, episode, or conversation being reported.
- The primary source and its date or journal/show identity.
- Whether clinicians are discussing it, represented by faces and a plain activity
  phrase rather than an engagement score.
- Whether the card contains an abstract, a playable moment, or a conversation.

## What expansion should reveal

- Source context: abstract, article description, FDA source, or episode moment.
- Physician conversation: original posts first, full bounded threads on demand,
  quote-post context, then classic repost amplification.
- Publisher context and related source links after physician conversation.
- Provenance labels that distinguish source facts, CanvasMD summaries, and quoted
  physician commentary.
